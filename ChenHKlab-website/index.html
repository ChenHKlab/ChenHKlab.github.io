Welcome to ChenHK Lab!


Research Mission.Our laboratory investigates the neurocognitive mechanisms underlying human language and its development, with a particular focus on syntactic processing. We seek to establish a casual understanding of how the brain represents and combines linguistic information into hierarchical structures. Through research on Chinese, bilingualism, and second language processing, we examine how language networks reflect both shared principles and language-specific characteristics. Our goal is to advance an account of the human language faculty that encompasses linguistic diversity.

Methodological Approach.We combine behavioral experiments, functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), and non-invasive neuromodulation, including transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS), transcranial photobiomodulation, and transcranial ultrasound stimulation. This integrated approach allows us to map language networks and test the causal contributions of specific brain regions to language processing. We develop artificial grammar and Chinese function-word-based paradigms to distinguish syntactic computation from semantic and associative processes. Connectivity analyses and computational modeling further characterize how brain regions interact to support language.

Translational Impact.Our research aims to connect fundamental discoveries about language and the brain with clinical, educational, and technological applications. By identifying neural mechanisms the support language processing, we lay the groundwork for neuromodulation-based interventions for language disorders. Our studies of bilingualism, second language learning, and AI-assisted writing from language education and the complementary use of human and artificial intelligence. We also promote knowledge dissemination through the translation of Language in our brain: the origins of a uniquely human capacity, making foundational insights in the neuroscience of language accessible to a wider Chinese-speaking readership. In the long term, our work provides a foundation for language brain-computer interfaces that support communication restoration and enhancement.
